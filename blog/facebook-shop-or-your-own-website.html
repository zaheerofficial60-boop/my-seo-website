Facebook Shop or Your Own Website: Where Should Your Business Live?

Many Pakistani sellers run their whole business from a Facebook or Instagram page. It works, until it doesn't. This comparison explains what social selling does well, what only a website can do, and how to use both.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · Online Business

For a huge number of small sellers, business begins on Facebook or Instagram. A page is quick to open, friends can share it, and the first orders arrive through Messenger and WhatsApp. It feels complete. Then, slowly, the limits appear: posts disappear under newer content, customers cannot find an old product, and everything depends on one platform's decisions. That is usually when the website question arrives: do I need my own site, or is social media enough?

This article compares the two honestly. Social selling is genuinely powerful, and a website is not magic. But they are built for different things, and understanding the difference helps you invest your time and money in the right order.

What Facebook and Instagram Selling Does Well

Social platforms are where people already spend their time, chatting, watching and discovering. A seller can show products in photos and short videos, answer questions in comments and messages, and build a following that feels personal. Live sessions and stories create a sense of a real shop with a real person, which suits products that benefit from demonstration and conversation, such as clothing, food and handmade goods.

Starting costs are low and feedback is instant. You learn within days which photos attract questions and which products people ask prices for. For testing a new idea, our guide to starting an online business in Pakistan treats a social page as a perfectly respectable first home.

The Hidden Limits of a Social-Only Business

A page you do not own is a shop on rented land. The platform decides how many of your followers see each post, can change its rules, and can restrict or close accounts, sometimes through automated mistakes that take time to appeal. Your whole customer connection sits inside that decision. Sellers who have lost a page know how quickly years of work can become unreachable.

Discovery is also limited in a second way: social content is hard to find later. A customer who saw your product last month cannot easily search for it again, and people searching Google for what you sell will mostly find websites, not your posts. Our comparison of social media or SEO for an online business explains this difference in detail. Add the difficulty of presenting full policies, complete ranges and detailed guides inside a feed, and the ceiling of a social-only business becomes clear.

What Your Own Website Adds

A website is an address that belongs to you. On it, your full range sits in a clear order, each product with its own page, complete description and honest policies. Those pages can be found through Google by people actively searching to buy, which is exactly what our ecommerce SEO service is designed to build. A good page keeps bringing visitors long after the day it was written, unlike a post that fades within days.

A website also changes how your business is judged. Customers share one clean address, printed on a card or a parcel, and find a business that looks established and easy to verify. For orders, enquiries and information, everything lives in one stable place that no platform update can rearrange.

The Honest Weaknesses of a Website

A website does not bring its own audience. A new site starts with no visitors, and building search traffic takes steady work over time. It also needs care: keeping products, prices and information updated, and making sure the site works well on the phones most customers use. A neglected website is worse than an active social page, because it suggests a business that may have closed.

There is also a setup cost in time or money that a social page does not have. That is a fair reason to sequence the two rather than starting both at once, especially while you are still proving that your product sells.

The Combination Most Sellers End Up With

In practice, growing sellers rarely abandon social media when they launch a website. They divide the jobs:

  • Social media for conversation, new product announcements, live selling and community.
  • The website for the complete catalogue, detailed information, policies and search visitors.
  • WhatsApp for personal order discussions, linked from both places.

Each social post can point to the fuller page on your site, and your site can point back to your active social profiles as proof of a living business. Built this way, the website becomes the permanent home and social media becomes its loudspeaker. Our article on Daraz or your own online store makes a similar point about marketplaces: rented reach and owned ground work best together.

Making the Decision at Your Stage

If you are testing an idea with almost no budget, start social, sell, learn and save. If orders are steady and you feel the limits described above, a website is your next serious step, and it should be built properly rather than as an afterthought. If you already have a website that nobody visits, the answer is not another platform but better pages and search work on the one you own.

Your business should live where you control its future. Use Facebook and Instagram for what they do brilliantly, and give your business a home of its own as soon as you reasonably can.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a Facebook page enough for a small business?

It can be enough to start, especially for testing products and reaching people who already use the platform. Over time, most growing businesses benefit from their own website as well, because it is searchable on Google and fully under their control.

What can a website do that a Facebook shop cannot?

A website can appear in Google searches with full product pages and guides, present your policies and story exactly as you choose, and stay yours regardless of platform changes. It is also a single address you can print on packaging and cards.

Do I have to stop selling on Facebook if I build a website?

No. Many sellers keep both: social media for conversation and discovery, and the website for search visitors, complete information and orders. Use each post and profile to point people towards your website.

Which should I build first if money is tight?

Start where it costs least and your customers already are, often a well-kept social page, and plan the website as your next step once the idea is proven. A simple website done well beats an expensive one done poorly.
