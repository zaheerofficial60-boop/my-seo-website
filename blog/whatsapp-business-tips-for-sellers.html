WhatsApp Business Tips for Sellers: Catalogue, Labels and Quick Replies

Many online sales in Pakistan happen inside a WhatsApp chat. Used casually, it becomes a mess of lost orders. Used properly with WhatsApp Business tools, it becomes an organised, professional shop counter.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · Online Business

For many small sellers, WhatsApp is not just a messaging app; it is the shop itself. Customers ask prices there, place orders there and chase deliveries there. Yet many sellers use a personal WhatsApp account with no profile information, no product list and no system, then wonder why enquiries get lost and customers get frustrated. The free WhatsApp Business app exists exactly to fix this, and its main tools are simple once someone explains them plainly.

This guide walks through the setup that matters for a seller: a complete business profile, a useful catalogue, labels that organise your orders, and quick replies that save you typing the same answers all day. None of it requires technical skill, just an hour of careful setup and the habit of keeping it tidy.

Set Up a Proper Business Profile

Start with the basics customers check before they trust you. Add your business name exactly as you use it everywhere else, a clear profile photo or logo, and a short description of what you sell. Fill in your location, your working hours and an email address if you have one. A complete profile tells a stranger that a real, organised business stands behind the number.

Also write a polite greeting message for people who message you for the first time, and an away message for hours when you cannot reply. These small touches set expectations honestly. A customer who receives a calm automatic note saying when you will reply is far less likely to assume you are ignoring them and buy elsewhere.

Build a Catalogue Customers Can Browse

The catalogue is your shelf inside WhatsApp. Instead of sending photos one by one in every chat, you add each main product once with a clear photo, its name, a short honest description and its price where you are comfortable showing it. Customers can then browse your range quietly, even outside your working hours, and message you about a specific item.

Keep the catalogue honest and current. Remove products you have stopped selling, update prices when they change, and use real photos of your own stock. A catalogue full of unavailable items creates the worst kind of conversation: a customer ready to buy something you cannot provide. A smaller, accurate catalogue serves you far better than a large, outdated one. The same discipline of clear product writing helps on your website too, which is why our ecommerce SEO service puts so much weight on accurate product pages.

Use Labels So No Order Gets Lost

As enquiries grow, memory alone fails. Labels solve this by letting you tag each chat with its stage. Sellers commonly use labels such as new enquiry, order confirmed, payment pending, packed, dispatched and delivered, plus one for customers who may buy again. The exact words do not matter; consistency does.

  • Label every new enquiry the moment it arrives.
  • Move the label as the order progresses, like a parcel moving through your shop.
  • Check the payment pending label each day and follow up politely.
  • Use a separate label for complaints or problems, so they get priority, not neglect.

With labels, opening WhatsApp in the morning becomes a clear to-do list instead of a long, worrying scroll. You can see which orders need packing, which customers are waiting for a reply, and which deliveries should have arrived.

Save Time With Quick Replies

Sellers type the same answers dozens of times: delivery charges, how to order, payment options, return conditions, shop timings. Quick replies let you save each answer once and insert it with a short shortcut instead of typing it again. This is faster for you and clearer for the customer, because the saved answer can be written carefully, once, instead of hastily in the middle of a busy day.

Write quick replies in the same friendly tone you would use yourself, in the language your customers prefer. Many sellers keep versions in both Urdu and English for common questions, a habit our article on Urdu and English content discusses for websites as well. Remember to personalise when it matters: a quick reply should start the answer, not replace human attention when a customer has a real problem.

Behave Like a Business in Every Chat

Tools help, but behaviour is what customers remember. Reply within the hours you have promised, confirm every order back in writing with the product, price and delivery details, and share dispatch updates without being asked twice. If a delivery is delayed, say so early and honestly. People forgive delays far more easily than silence.

Keep business and personal chats separate if you can, ideally with the Business app on its own number. It protects your privacy, keeps customer records together, and makes the business easier to hand over to a helper as you grow. Our guide to running a home-based online business has more on keeping this kind of daily organisation in place.

Connect WhatsApp With Your Other Channels

WhatsApp works best as one part of a joined-up presence. Put your WhatsApp contact option on your website, social media pages and packaging so interested people can reach you in one tap. At the same time, guide happy WhatsApp customers towards your website and pages, where they can see your full range and find you again through Google search. Each channel supports the others, and no single app holds your whole business hostage.

Set up properly, WhatsApp Business turns a chaotic chat list into a calm, professional selling system. An hour spent on your profile, catalogue, labels and quick replies will save you time every single day that follows.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is WhatsApp Business free to use for sellers?

The WhatsApp Business app itself is widely used by small sellers without a subscription for its basic tools like the profile, catalogue, labels and quick replies. Always check the current app details yourself, as features can change over time.

What should I put in my WhatsApp catalogue?

Add your main products with a clear photo, a short honest description and the price where you are comfortable showing it. Keep the catalogue updated: remove items you no longer sell so customers are not disappointed.

How do labels help a seller?

Labels let you sort chats by stage, such as new enquiry, order confirmed, payment pending or delivered. Instead of scrolling through every conversation, you can see at a glance which customers need a reply or a follow-up.

Should I message customers who have not contacted me first?

Be careful and respectful. Messaging people who never asked to hear from you feels like spam and can harm your reputation. Focus on replying well to people who contact you, and on customers who have clearly agreed to updates.
