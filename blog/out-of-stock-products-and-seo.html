Out-of-Stock Products and SEO: What to Do With Unavailable Pages

Every store runs out of things. The question is what happens to the product page when it does. This guide explains the sensible options for temporarily unavailable and permanently discontinued products, and why deleting in a hurry is usually the wrong one.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · Ecommerce SEO

Stock changes are a normal part of selling. A popular item sells out before the next delivery, a supplier discontinues a line, a seasonal product reaches the end of its season. What is not normal, or wise, is treating the product page as disposable the moment the shelf empties. A page that has been indexed by Google, linked from your categories and perhaps bookmarked or shared by customers has value. How you handle it affects both the shoppers who still arrive there and the search visibility you worked to build.

The right choice depends on one simple question: is this product coming back? This guide separates the temporary case from the permanent one, so you can build a simple policy for your store. It is one of the practical housekeeping areas our ecommerce SEO service helps store owners put in order.

Temporarily Out of Stock: Keep the Page, Be Honest

If the product will return, keep the page live and say clearly that the item is currently out of stock. Where your platform allows it, offer a notification option so interested shoppers can hear when it returns, or allow pre-orders only if you can fulfil them fairly. Show genuinely similar alternatives on the page, so the visit is not wasted. Most importantly, keep the page's stock information accurate in the product data itself, not just in a banner, so search engines and shoppers read the same honest story.

While the item is away, consider where it appears in your category listings. A sold-out product sitting at the top of its category frustrates browsers who cannot buy it. Many stores move unavailable items lower in the listing or note the status clearly on the listing itself, following the category principles in our category page SEO guide.

Discontinued Products: Choose Between Redirect and Removal

When a product is never coming back, you have three sensible options. If a close replacement exists, redirect the old page address to the replacement, so visitors and search engines land on the most useful alternative. If there is no replacement but the product belongs to a strong category, redirecting to that category can also work, provided the category genuinely serves the same need. If the product had little search interest, few links and no natural successor, letting the page be removed is acceptable, ideally with a helpful error page that guides visitors to your main categories.

What you should avoid is redirecting every dead product to your homepage. A visitor who wanted a specific item gains nothing from a generic homepage, and search engines treat such redirects as a poor match. Choose the destination the shopper would have wanted.

Special Cases: Seasonal and Variant Products

Seasonal products deserve their own rule. If an item returns every year, keep its page between seasons, marked clearly as unavailable for now, and refresh it when the season approaches. Rebuilding the same page from scratch each year throws away its history and delays its return to search results. Variants need care too: if one colour or size sells out but others remain, the product page stays, with the unavailable option clearly marked rather than quietly selectable at checkout.

Also review bundled or related-product sections. A bundle that includes an unavailable item, or a related products row full of sold-out pages, makes the whole store look neglected even when the main product is fine.

Keep Shoppers Informed at Every Step

Whatever option you choose, communication is the thread that protects trust. State the status plainly on the page, avoid showing a buy button for something that cannot be bought, and if a customer ordered shortly before stock ran out, contact them quickly with an honest choice: wait, swap or refund. Search engines notice behaviour too; pages where visitors arrive, find a dead end and leave immediately do not build confidence in your store.

A well-kept out-of-stock page can even keep working for you. It holds the page's place in search, offers alternatives that may suit the visitor, and captures interest for the product's return. That is far better than the error page a deleted product leaves behind.

Make It a Written Policy, Not a Guess

Stores handle stock changes badly when each case is improvised by whoever notices first. Write a short policy instead: temporary gaps keep the page with a clear notice, returning seasonal items stay live between seasons, discontinued items redirect to the closest match, and only pages with no interest and no successor are removed. Share the policy with anyone who updates the store, and review your sold-out pages regularly to check the rules are being followed. Small, consistent habits like this are what separate a maintained store from a slowly decaying one, and they feature in the wider clean-up described in common ecommerce SEO mistakes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I delete a product page as soon as it sells out?

Usually not. If the product is coming back, keep the page, mark it clearly as out of stock and offer a way to register interest or view alternatives. Deleting and recreating pages wastes the visibility the original page built and confuses returning visitors.

What is a redirect and when should I use one?

A redirect automatically sends visitors and search engines from an old page address to a new one. Use it when a discontinued product has a close replacement, so people following old links arrive somewhere useful instead of an error page.

Will out-of-stock pages hurt my store in search?

A few honestly marked out-of-stock pages are a normal part of running a store. Problems grow when a large share of the store is unavailable, dates and information are wrong, or sold-out items dominate category pages ahead of products people can actually buy.

What should I do with seasonal products after the season ends?

If the product returns each season, keep the page, mark it clearly out of season and bring it back when stock returns. The page keeps its history and can be refreshed with new details instead of starting again from nothing every year.
