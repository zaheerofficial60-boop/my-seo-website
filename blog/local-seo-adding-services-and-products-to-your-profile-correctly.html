Adding Services and Products to Your Profile

A business profile is often the first place a customer checks before calling or visiting. When services and products are listed clearly, customers can quickly see whether a business can help them. When the list is vague, incomplete or out of date, customers may move on to another business. This guide explains how to add services and products correctly, in simple steps that any small business can follow.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 29 October 2026 · LOCAL SEO

Start With the Services You Actually Offer

Before adding anything, make a simple list of the services your business truly offers today. Write each service in plain words, exactly as a customer would describe it, and leave out anything you no longer provide. This honest list becomes the basis for your profile, and it prevents confusion later when a customer asks for something you cannot deliver. A short, accurate list is far more useful than a long list filled with guesses. A quick review with someone who helps in the business can reveal a service you forgot to include.

It also helps to think about how customers ask for each service. A customer may use a simple everyday word while the business uses a technical term, so choose the simpler wording for the profile. If a service has different forms, such as a repair, a check-up or a complete replacement, describe each one separately where the profile allows it. Clear wording helps customers recognise the service they need without having to call just to ask what it means.

Describe Each Service in Simple Words

A good service description answers three questions: what the service is, who it is for, and what the customer should do next. Keep the description short and practical, and avoid phrases that promise results you cannot guarantee. For example, explain the steps involved, whether an appointment is needed, and whether the customer should bring anything along. Customers appreciate this kind of detail because it helps them prepare and decide with confidence. If a customer still asks what a service means, rewrite that description in even simpler words.

Avoid copying descriptions from other businesses or using long, formal language that sounds impressive but says little. Write as if you are explaining the service to a customer standing in your shop. If a service is only available in certain circumstances, say so plainly. Honest descriptions build trust, and they reduce the number of calls from customers who expected something different from what the business actually provides.

Add Products Only Where They Fit

Some businesses sell products as well as services, and a profile may allow both to be listed. Add a product only if you actually stock it or can arrange it reliably, and describe it in the same simple way as a service. State what the product is, what it is used for in general terms, and how a customer can ask about availability. Do not list products you hope to stock one day, because customers may visit or call expecting to find them. When a product returns to stock after a gap, update the profile promptly so customers are not misled.

It is also important to keep product information separate from service information where the profile gives you that choice. Mixing the two can confuse customers who are looking for one or the other. If a product is linked to a service, such as a part used in a repair, mention that connection briefly in the service description instead of listing the same item twice. A tidy profile is easier to read, easier to update, and easier for customers to trust.

Use Categories and Details Carefully

Most profiles ask you to choose a category for your business, and some allow extra details for each service. Choose the category that best matches your main work, rather than a broad category that covers everything. If the profile allows extra details, such as whether a service is offered at the customer's location, fill in only the details you are sure about. Leaving a detail blank is better than guessing, because a wrong detail can mislead a customer. If you are unsure about a category, choose the closest honest match rather than a broader misleading one.

Check also whether the profile asks for a general description of the business. Use this space to explain, in a few sentences, what the business does and who it serves, without repeating every service. The individual service entries can carry the detail, while the general description gives the overall picture. When categories, descriptions and service entries agree with each other, customers get a consistent message wherever they look on the profile.

Keep the List Updated as Things Change

A profile is not finished once services and products are added. Businesses change over time: a service may be added, another may be stopped, and a product may go out of stock for a long period. Review the list regularly, perhaps once a month, and remove anything that is no longer offered. Add new services only after you are ready to provide them, and update descriptions when the process changes in a way customers should know about. A note on your calendar for this monthly check makes it far more likely to happen.

Updating also means checking that the same information appears correctly elsewhere, such as on your website. If a service is removed from the profile but still appears on the website, customers will be confused about which version is correct. A short monthly check of the profile and the website together keeps both in agreement. This habit takes little time, but it protects the business from lost calls, wasted visits and disappointed customers.

Check the Profile as a Customer Would

After adding or updating services and products, open the profile on a phone and read it as a customer would. Can you understand each service quickly? Is it clear how to contact the business? Does anything look out of date, repeated or confusing? If you struggle to understand a service, a customer will struggle too, so rewrite that entry in simpler words. This simple check often reveals small problems that are easy to fix. Small fixes made after this check often improve the profile more than a complete rewrite would.

Finally, ask a friend or a regular customer to look at the profile and tell you what is unclear. A fresh pair of eyes often notices wording that the owner has stopped seeing. Make the suggested fixes, save the changes, and check again after a short time to confirm they appear correctly. A profile that is clear, honest and up to date helps customers choose with confidence, and it supports the good service the business provides in person.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should a business list every service it has ever offered?

No, list only the services you offer today, so customers are not misled by outdated or unavailable options.

How long should a service description be?

Long enough to explain what the service is, who it is for and what the customer should do next, in simple everyday words.

How often should services and products on a profile be reviewed?

Review them at least once a month, and update them whenever a service or product is added, changed or stopped.
