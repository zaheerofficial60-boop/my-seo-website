KEYWORD RESEARCH

Free Keyword Research Methods That Actually Work

You do not need a paid subscription to find good keywords. These free methods, straight from Google and your own customers, give you evidence you can trust.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · Keyword Research

Many business owners assume keyword research requires paid software with a monthly bill. Paid tools can save time, but they are not the entry ticket. Some of the most reliable keyword evidence is free, because it comes directly from Google and from your own customers. This guide gathers the free methods that genuinely work, so you can build a useful keyword list with nothing but a browser, a spreadsheet, and attention.

A word of honesty first: free methods will not give you exact search numbers for every phrase. Treat any precise-looking figures from free sources with care. What free methods give you instead is direction: which phrases people use, which questions they ask, and which topics your own visitors already find you for. For most small businesses, that direction is enough to plan well, and it is exactly how a lean keyword research and strategy service begins before any deeper analysis.

Google Autocomplete: Suggestions as You Type

Start typing a service phrase into Google and pause. The suggestions that appear are drawn from real searches by real people, which makes them a free window into demand. Work through the alphabet trick: type your service followed by each letter in turn and note the relevant completions. Also try adding words like price, near, best, how, and why before or after your service to surface different kinds of phrases. Record everything relevant in your spreadsheet.

Related Searches and People Also Ask

After running a search, scroll through the results page. The People Also Ask boxes contain real questions searchers pose, each one a potential blog topic or FAQ entry, and expanding them reveals still more questions. At the bottom of the page, related searches list neighbouring phrases Google considers closely connected. Together, these two free features can expand one seed idea into a full cluster of topics for a service page and several supporting articles.

Google Search Console: Your Own Real Data

If your website is verified in Google Search Console, you already own the most honest keyword source of all: the actual queries your pages appeared for, with the pages they belong to. Look for queries where you appear but not near the top. Those are opportunities, because Google already connects your page with that phrase. Also look for unexpected queries that brought visitors, which often reveal wording you would never have guessed. Reviewing this free report regularly turns keyword research from guessing into responding.

Google Trends and Seasonal Clues

Google Trends is free and shows how interest in a phrase rises and falls over time and differs between regions. It will not tell you exact volumes, but it is excellent for spotting seasonal patterns and comparing two ways of phrasing the same service. If one wording is clearly more popular in your region, prefer it. Trends is also the right starting point when planning content ahead of busy seasons, a topic we cover fully in seasonal keywords and planning ahead.

Your Customers, Reviews, and Messages

The most overlooked free source is your own business. The questions customers ask by phone, the words they use in messages, and the language in their reviews are keyword research in raw form. When several customers describe the same need in the same words, that phrase belongs on your list. Keep a running note on your phone for a month and you will collect phrases no tool database contains, because they come from your town and your trade.

  • Ask new customers: what did you search to find us?
  • Save the exact wording of common questions.
  • Mine reviews for the benefits customers mention most.
  • Check questions asked on your Google Business Profile.

Competitor Pages and YouTube Suggestions

Reading the titles and headings of competing businesses costs nothing and reveals which phrases the market targets. YouTube search suggestions are another free goldmine for how-to and informational topics, since many people search YouTube the way they search Google. If you plan articles or FAQs, a few minutes collecting YouTube completions for your services will give you question ideas in the exact words learners use. Combine these with the step order in how to do keyword research step by step and you have a complete free system.

Putting the Free Methods Together

No single free source tells the whole story, but together they triangulate the truth. A phrase that appears in autocomplete, shows up in related searches, matches customer questions, and already brings impressions in Search Console is a safe, evidence-backed target. Build your list that way, phrase by phrase, and you will have research most competitors never bother to do, without spending anything except time and attention.

Keep your findings in one spreadsheet from the very first day, with the source of each phrase noted beside it. That small habit prevents confusion later, because you can see at a glance whether a keyword came from real visitor data, from Google suggestions, or from a customer conversation, and judge how much weight to give it when you plan pages.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I really do keyword research for free?

Yes. Google autocomplete, related searches, People Also Ask, Search Console, Google Trends, competitor pages, and your own customer questions provide enough evidence to build a solid list.

What is the most reliable free keyword source?

Google Search Console is the most honest source for an existing website, because it shows the real queries your own pages already appear for and the visitors they bring.

Do free methods show exact search volumes?

Generally no. Free methods show direction and real wording rather than precise numbers, so treat any exact figures from free sources with caution and rely on combined evidence.

How often should I repeat free keyword research?

Review Search Console and customer questions every month or two, and refresh your full list whenever you add services or plan seasonal content.
