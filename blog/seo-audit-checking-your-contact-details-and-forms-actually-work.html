Checking Your Contact Details and Forms Work

A website can look good and still fail at the most important step: letting customers contact the business. A wrong phone number, a form that does not send, or a link that leads nowhere can lose a customer who was ready to ask a question. This guide gives a simple audit checklist to test contact details and forms properly, before customers discover the problem themselves.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 29 October 2026 · SEO AUDITS

Start With the Details Customers See First

Begin the audit with the contact details shown most prominently: the phone number, email address and business address on the homepage, header and footer. Check that the phone number is correct digit by digit, that the email address is spelled correctly, and that the address matches the real location of the business. Small typing errors are easy to miss when reading your own website, yet they can prevent every customer who tries to use that detail from reaching you.

Also check that these details are consistent across the website. The phone number in the footer should match the number on the contact page and the number on the business profile. If different numbers appear in different places, customers will not know which one to trust. A short check of the main pages, done slowly and carefully, often finds inconsistencies that a quick glance would miss, and fixing them is usually simple once they are noticed.

Test Phone Numbers and Email Links Properly

Seeing a phone number on a page is not enough; it must also work when tapped. Open the website on a real phone, tap the phone number, and confirm that it opens the calling screen with the correct number. Do the same for email links, and check that they open a new message addressed correctly. If a number is shown as plain text that cannot be tapped, many mobile customers will not bother to copy it by hand, and the business may lose their call.

It is also worth calling the number yourself and sending a test email to the address shown, to confirm both are monitored. A number that rings without answer during business hours, or an email inbox that is rarely checked, is a practical problem, not just a website problem. Note how long it takes to get a response, and whether the reply is helpful. Customers judge a business by this experience, so contact details should lead to a real, responsive person, not just a correct-looking page.

Submit Every Form as a Customer Would

Forms are a common point of failure, because they can look complete while failing to send anything. Fill in every form on the website exactly as a customer would, using realistic details and a message similar to a genuine enquiry. Submit the form, and then check whether a confirmation message appears, whether an email arrives, and whether the details are recorded correctly. If any step fails, note exactly where it failed, so the problem can be fixed without guessing.

Repeat this test for each form, because different forms may behave differently. A contact form, a booking request and a newsletter signup may each have their own settings, and a problem in one does not mean the others work. Also test the form on a phone, where small fields, unclear labels or buttons that are hard to tap can cause customers to give up. A form that is easy to complete on a computer but frustrating on a phone will still lose many enquiries.

Check Error Messages and Required Fields

A good form helps customers when they make a mistake, rather than punishing them for it. Test what happens when a required field is left blank, when an email address is typed incorrectly, or when a message is very short. The form should show a clear, polite message explaining what needs to be fixed, and it should keep the details the customer already typed. If the form clears everything and forces the customer to start again, many people will simply leave.

Also check that required fields are genuinely necessary. Asking for too much information before a customer can send a simple question creates friction and may discourage enquiries. Keep forms short, ask only for what is needed to reply helpfully, and explain why any sensitive detail is requested. A form that respects the customer's time and privacy is more likely to be completed, and the enquiries it produces are usually clearer and easier to answer.

Follow the Journey After Submission

An audit should not stop at the submit button. Check what happens after a customer sends a form: do they see a clear confirmation, do they receive an acknowledgement, and does someone in the business know a new enquiry has arrived? If enquiries sit unnoticed in an inbox or a dashboard, the form may be working technically while failing in practice. Assign responsibility for checking enquiries regularly, and keep a simple routine for replying promptly and politely.

It is also useful to check the information that arrives with each enquiry. Does it include the customer's name, contact details and message in a readable form? Is anything cut off, garbled or missing? Poorly formatted enquiry details waste time and can lead to replies sent to the wrong address. A short end-to-end test, from submission to reply, reveals problems that a visual check of the form alone would never show, and it confirms that the whole contact journey works.

Repeat the Audit Regularly

Contact details and forms should be audited regularly, not only when a problem is reported. Set a simple routine, perhaps once a month, to re-check phone numbers, email links, addresses and every form on the website. Also repeat the audit after any website change, however small, because an update to one page can accidentally break a link or a form on another page. A short, regular check is far cheaper than the lost enquiries caused by a fault that goes unnoticed for weeks.

Keep a brief record of each audit: the date, what was tested, and what was fixed. This record helps the next check go faster, and it shows whether the same problem keeps returning. Over time, regular audits build confidence that customers can always reach the business, which is the foundation of good online service. A website that is easy to contact, with details and forms that truly work, supports every other part of a business's online presence.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should contact details and forms be audited?

At least once a month, and again after any website change, because small updates can accidentally break links or forms.

Is it enough to check that a phone number looks correct?

No, tap it on a real phone, call it yourself, and confirm it reaches a monitored number that is answered helpfully.

What makes a website form easy for customers to use?

Short fields, clear labels, helpful error messages that keep typed details, and a clear confirmation after submission.
