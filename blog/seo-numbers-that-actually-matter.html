SEO Numbers That Actually Matter for Your Business

Your reports are full of numbers, but only a few of them connect to customers and income. This guide separates the numbers worth watching from the vanity metrics that mainly look impressive.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · Analytics & Reporting

Ask a business owner how their SEO is going and you often hear a number: visits are up, a keyword is at position three, impressions doubled. Numbers feel solid, but not all numbers are equal. Some describe real customers moving towards you. Others only describe activity on a screen. Learning the difference saves money, arguments and bad decisions.

This guide ranks the common SEO numbers by how closely they connect to your business, and shows you how to build a short monthly list you can actually use.

Start From Enquiries and Work Backwards

The closest numbers to income are enquiries: phone calls from your website and Google profile, contact form submissions, messages and direction requests from people planning to visit. When our analytics, tracking and reporting service builds a report, these come first, because everything else in SEO exists to support them.

Work backwards from there. Enquiries come from visits, visits come from clicks, and clicks come from appearing in search results. Each step back is still useful, but it is one step further from money, so it deserves slightly less weight in your judgement.

Clicks, Search Visits and Your Best Pages

Clicks from Google search, shown in Search Console, matter because they are real people choosing your result over others. Search visits in Analytics matter for the same reason, especially when you look at which pages received them. A service page gaining search visits month by month is quietly becoming an asset. An article that brings steady visits on a problem your customers have is doing real work too.

  • Calls and forms: The strongest sign SEO is producing business.
  • Search clicks: People actively choosing you in Google results.
  • Top pages by search visits: Shows which content earns its keep.
  • Direction requests: For local businesses, a strong intent signal.

Review these together monthly, as described in how to read an SEO report, rather than celebrating any one of them alone.

The Vanity Metrics to Treat Carefully

Vanity metrics are not lies; they are numbers presented without their context. Total impressions can rise simply because you appeared far down the results for many searches nobody clicked. A single keyword position, checked on one phone in one town, says little about what customers elsewhere see. Raw visitor counts that mix search, social, accidental and repeat visits into one big number hide more than they reveal.

The test is simple: if this number doubled next month, would you expect more enquiries? If the honest answer is not really, treat it as background. Providers who lead with vanity metrics and bury enquiry numbers are choosing the numbers that flatter them.

Why Context Changes What a Number Means

The same number can be good news in one month and a warning in another. Fewer calls in a traditionally quiet season may match your real shop footfall exactly. A sudden traffic rise from a country you do not serve is not growth. A fall after removing duplicate pages can be healthy tidying, not decline.

This is why honest reporting compares like with like: this month against the previous month, and for seasonal businesses, against the same period last year where data exists. It also explains changes instead of hiding them. Our guide on measuring local SEO success goes deeper on judging local actions such as calls and direction requests fairly.

Building Your Short Monthly List

Write down five numbers and review only those each month: calls from Google and your website, form or message enquiries, direction requests, search clicks, and your three best pages by search visits. Keep them in one simple sheet with the month beside them. Within a few months you will see your own pattern, including your busy and quiet periods, and outside claims will be easier to judge.

If tracking is not set up to show calls and enquiries separately, fix that before anything else. You cannot value what you do not count, and counting the right things is a one-time setup task with lasting benefits.

Judge Trends, Not Days

Daily numbers jump around for ordinary reasons: weather, weekdays, local events and Google's own adjustments. Owners who check daily often panic, change direction, and never give any approach time to work. Monthly trends, read calmly alongside your real enquiry diary, give a truer picture.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a vanity metric in SEO?

A vanity metric is a number that looks good in a report but does not connect to enquiries or sales, such as total impressions alone, follower-style counts, or a single keyword position checked once. It can create a feeling of progress without any real business change.

Is website traffic a vanity metric?

Traffic on its own is incomplete, not useless. Visits matter when you know where they came from and what visitors did next. Traffic from Google search that leads to calls and enquiries is valuable; unexplained traffic that never enquiries is a question, not a success.

How many numbers should I track each month?

A short list works best for most small businesses: search clicks, calls, direction requests, form enquiries, and your best pages. Five honest numbers reviewed monthly teach you more than thirty numbers you never have time to understand.

Why do my keyword positions change so often?

Positions vary by town, device, time and the searcher, and Google constantly adjusts results. Judge your important keywords over weeks and months as part of overall visibility, and weigh clicks and enquiries more heavily than any single position check.
