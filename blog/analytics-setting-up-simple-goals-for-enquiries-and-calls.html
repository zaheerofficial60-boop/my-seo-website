Setting Simple Goals for Enquiries and Calls

Many small business websites get visitors, but the owner is not sure whether those visitors ever become customers. A simple goal in Google Analytics can answer that question. When you track enquiries and calls, you can see which pages help your business and which pages only attract passing traffic.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 28 October 2026 · ANALYTICS

Start With What Counts as an Enquiry

An enquiry is any clear step a visitor takes to contact your business. It may be a form submission, a tap on your phone number, a tap on your email address, or a visit to your contact page followed by a message. Before you set up anything in Analytics, write down the two or three actions that matter most for your business. For most small service businesses, a phone call and a form enquiry are the two main actions worth tracking.

Keeping this list short is important. If you try to track everything at once, your reports become hard to read and easy to ignore. Start with enquiries and calls only. You can add other actions later, once you are comfortable reading the basic reports. A clear list also helps you explain the results to anyone who works on your website, because everyone will agree on what success looks like.

Use One Clear Contact Form

Your contact form should be simple, with only the fields you truly need. A name, a phone number or email address, and a short message are usually enough for a first enquiry. Long forms can discourage people who are ready to ask a question but do not want to share too much detail at the start. Make sure the form works well on a mobile phone, because many visitors in Pakistan browse on their phones.

After a visitor submits the form, show a clear thank-you page with its own address, such as a page called thank-you. This separate page is what makes goal tracking easy. In Google Analytics, you can set a goal that counts every visit to that thank-you page as one enquiry. Test the form yourself before you rely on the numbers, and check that the thank-you page appears every time.

A good thank-you page also helps the visitor. It should confirm that the message was received, explain what happens next in plain words, and give the phone number again in case the visitor prefers to call. This small step builds trust, because the visitor does not have to wonder whether the form worked. It also gives you a clean, separate page address to track, which keeps your enquiry numbers accurate and easy to explain.

Track Phone Calls as a Separate Goal

Phone calls are often more important than form submissions for local businesses, so they deserve their own goal. If your phone number is a clickable link on your website, Analytics can record taps on that link as an event. This helps you see how many people tried to call, even if you cannot see every completed call in Analytics itself. Keep a simple notebook or phone log beside this data, so you can compare website taps with the calls you actually receive.

It is also useful to place your phone number where visitors can find it without searching. Put it in the header, near the top of service pages, and again near the contact form. When the number is easy to find, more visitors will use it, and your call data will give a fairer picture of interest. Avoid changing the number often, because a changed number can break links and confuse returning visitors.

Set Up the Goal Step by Step

In Google Analytics, a goal is simply a rule that says what counts as a success. For a form enquiry, the rule can be a visit to your thank-you page. For a phone tap, the rule can be a click on the phone link. Give each goal a clear name, such as Form Enquiry or Phone Tap, so the report is easy to understand later. Write down the exact rule you used, because you may need it when you check the setup again in a few months.

After you create a goal, test it the same day. Submit a test enquiry and tap the phone link yourself, then check whether Analytics recorded those actions. Testing may take some time to appear in reports, so do not worry if the result is not instant. If a test action is missing, check the page address, the link, and the Analytics tag on that page before changing the goal itself.

Read the Report Once a Week

A weekly check is enough for most small businesses. Look at how many form enquiries and phone taps you received, and which pages sent visitors to those actions. A page that brings many enquiries is doing useful work, even if it does not have the highest traffic. A page with many visitors but no enquiries may need clearer wording, a simpler form, or a more visible phone number.

Do not judge a single week too harshly. One quiet week does not mean your website has failed, and one busy week does not mean every problem is solved. Look for a pattern over several weeks. If the same page keeps bringing enquiries, learn from it and apply the same clear structure to other pages. If a page never brings enquiries, small changes to its heading, first paragraph, or contact section are a better first step than a full rewrite.

When you review the report, also note where visitors came from, such as search, social media, or a direct visit. This does not need a deep analysis. A simple note like “most enquiries came from search this week” is enough to guide your next steps. Over time, you will see which sources bring people who actually contact you, rather than people who only look around and leave.

Improve One Thing at a Time

When you find a weak page, change one thing and watch the next few weeks of data. You might shorten the form, move the phone number higher on the page, or rewrite the opening paragraph so the service is clear at once. Changing one thing at a time helps you know what made a difference. If you change five things together, you will not know which change helped and which change caused a problem.

Keep your notes simple. Record the date, the change you made, and the goal numbers before and after. Over time, these notes become a useful guide for your whole website. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need advanced skills to set up a simple goal in Google Analytics?

No, a basic goal for a thank-you page or a phone link tap can be set up with simple steps, and testing it once will confirm it works.

How long should I wait before judging my goal results?

Give it a few weeks, because one week alone can be quiet or busy for reasons that have nothing to do with your website.

Should I track form enquiries and phone calls separately?

Yes, tracking them separately shows whether visitors prefer to call or to write, which helps you improve the right contact option.

What should I do if my goal shows no enquiries at all?

First test the form and phone link yourself, then check that the Analytics tag is on the page and that the goal rule matches the correct page or link.
