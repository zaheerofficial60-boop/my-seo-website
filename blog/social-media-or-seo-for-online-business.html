Social Media or SEO for an Online Business: Where Each One Fits

Sellers often ask whether they should spend their limited time on social media or on SEO. The honest answer is that they do different jobs. This guide explains where each fits in a growing online business.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · Online Business

A new online seller has limited hours, so every marketing choice feels like a trade-off. Should you spend the evening making social media posts, or improving your website for Google search? People online argue strongly for one side or the other, usually because they are selling that service. The calmer truth is that social media and SEO are not rivals. They reach customers at different moments, and a healthy online business usually needs both, in the right order.

This article explains what each channel is genuinely good at, where each is weak, and how a small seller in Pakistan can combine them without burning out.

What Social Media Does Best

Social media is a place of discovery and conversation. People scroll without a fixed buying plan, and a good photo, a short video or a helpful post can put your product in front of someone who was not looking for it. It is also fast: you can announce a new item today and receive enquiries today. For a brand new seller with no website traffic at all, that immediacy is precious, which is why our guide to getting your first online customers puts social presence early in the plan.

Social media is also where trust becomes personal. Customers see your face, hear your voice, watch you pack an order and read how you reply to questions. That human contact suits the way many buyers in Pakistan prefer to shop: chat first, ask questions, then order.

Where Social Media Is Weak

The weakness is that social attention is rented and short-lived. A post is usually seen for a brief period, then buried under newer content. If you stop posting, enquiries often slow down quickly. Your reach also depends on each platform's rules and systems, which change without asking you. Accounts can be restricted, trends shift, and yesterday's reliable channel can quieten.

There is another limit: people on social media are not always ready to buy. Many are browsing for entertainment, so turning views into orders takes repeated effort. A seller who depends only on social media must keep feeding it constantly just to stand still.

What SEO Does Best

SEO is about being found at the moment of intention. When someone searches Google for a product or a solution, they already want something. A store page that matches that search can bring a visitor who is far closer to buying than a casual scroller. This is the core strength of our ecommerce SEO service: building product and category pages that answer real searches clearly and honestly.

SEO also builds an asset. A helpful product page or guide keeps working month after month, bringing visitors without you posting daily. Unlike a social post, a good page does not disappear tomorrow. Over time, this steady search presence becomes one of the most reliable sources of customers a small business can own.

Where SEO Is Weak

SEO's weakness is speed. A new page takes time to be found, understood and ranked, and nobody honest can promise a particular position or date. Sellers who need sales this week cannot rely on SEO alone. SEO also requires a website worth ranking: clear pages, honest descriptions and a store that works well on phones. Without that foundation, there is nothing for search engines to reward.

It is also ongoing work, though of a different kind: improving pages, adding useful content and keeping information accurate. The effort is steadier and quieter than social media, but it is not zero, and our overview of online shopping and SEO in Pakistan shows why this groundwork matters more as buying moves online.

A Sensible Order for a Small Seller

For most new sellers, a practical sequence looks like this:

  1. Use social media and personal contacts to get your first enquiries and learn what customers ask.
  2. Build or improve your own store pages using those real questions and words.
  3. Keep posting socially, but let each good idea also become lasting website content.
  4. As search visitors grow, balance your time between the two channels.

Notice how the channels feed each other. A question asked in your social comments becomes a section on your product page. A guide written for your website becomes several social posts. One idea, used twice, halves the workload problem that makes sellers feel they must choose.

Judge Each Channel by the Right Measure

Finally, measure each channel by what it is for. Judge social media by conversations, enquiries and repeat contact, not only by follower counts. Judge SEO by whether search visitors and the sales they bring grow over the months, not by daily movements. Sellers get discouraged when they expect social media to behave like a permanent asset, or SEO to behave like an instant announcement board.

Social media starts the conversation today; SEO makes sure customers can find you tomorrow. An online business that understands both, and builds them in a sensible order, does not have to choose between them at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is faster, social media or SEO?

Social media is usually faster to start conversations because you can post today and people may see it today. SEO is slower because search engines need time to find, understand and rank your pages, but the visitors it brings later are actively searching to buy.

Can social media replace a website and SEO completely?

For a very small start it can carry you for a while, but it leaves your business dependent on platforms you do not control. A website with SEO gives you a permanent home that searchers can find even when social trends and rules change.

I have very little time. Which one should I choose first?

Start with the channel where your customers already spend time and where you can be consistent, often social media for a new seller. At the same time, keep your product pages and basic information search-friendly so SEO can grow in the background.

Does posting on social media directly improve my Google ranking?

Social posts do not work like a direct ranking switch. They help indirectly by bringing visitors, building your name and earning mentions, while your rankings themselves depend mainly on your website pages, their quality and their relevance to searches.
