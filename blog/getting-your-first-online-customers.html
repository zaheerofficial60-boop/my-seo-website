Getting Your First Online Customers: Practical Ways That Actually Work

The hardest customers are the first ones, because nobody knows you yet. This guide shares practical, honest ways to earn those early sales without fake reviews, bought followers or empty promises.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · Online Business

Opening an online store is the easy part. The difficult moment comes afterwards, when the page is live, the products are listed, and nobody is buying. Many new sellers respond by chasing shortcuts: buying followers, copying a bigger seller's photos, or paying for reviews that never happened. Those tricks create an appearance of success while quietly destroying the trust a new business needs most.

There is a slower, honest route to your first customers, and it works because it is built on how people actually decide to buy from a stranger. This guide goes through it step by step, from the people closest to you outwards to search and social media.

Start With People Who Already Trust You

Your first customers rarely come from the whole internet. They come from your existing circle: customers of your physical shop, neighbours, relatives, colleagues and community contacts. These people already believe you are a real person who will deliver what was promised. Tell them clearly, in person and through your usual channels, that you are now selling online, what you are selling and how to order.

Treat these early orders with extra care. Pack them well, deliver on time and follow up to ask whether everything arrived as expected. A happy early customer does more than pay you once: they tell others, they leave a genuine review when asked, and they give you honest feedback about prices, photos and delivery that strangers never will.

Make It Easy to Find and Judge You

Before spending effort on promotion, make sure a stranger who finds you can quickly answer three questions: what do you sell, what does it cost, and how do I reach you? If any answer is hidden, visitors leave. Complete every profile you use, keep your business name the same everywhere, and show clear photos and prices instead of asking everyone to message for basic information.

This is also where helpful written content earns its keep. A short guide answering a question your customers always ask gives people a reason to find you and a reason to trust you. Planning that kind of writing is exactly what our content strategy and copywriting service covers, because for a new store, useful words often do the work an advertising budget cannot.

Be Present Where Your Customers Already Gather

Go where conversations about your kind of product already happen. That may be local community groups, marketplace searches, or social media pages followed by people in your town and beyond. Join in helpfully first: answer questions, share useful knowledge, and only mention your products when it genuinely fits. People remember the seller who helped them long after they forget the seller who only posted adverts.

If you run a physical location as well, set up your Google Business Profile properly so nearby searchers can find you, and invite your in-person customers to follow your online shop. Our article on social media or SEO for an online business explains how these channels support each other rather than competing.

Earn Trust Signals Honestly

Strangers look for proof that others bought from you and were treated well. You cannot manufacture that proof, but you can earn it faster by asking properly. After a successful delivery, politely ask the customer to leave a review where you sell, and make the process easy for them. Share real photos of orders being packed and delivered, with the customer's permission where a person is shown.

  • Ask for reviews only after a real, completed order.
  • Reply to every review, including critical ones, calmly and helpfully.
  • Show your delivery and returns terms openly, before people ask.
  • Keep your promises on dispatch times, even if that means promising a slower, honest time.

Give People a Reason to Choose You First

A small new seller cannot usually win on being cheapest, and trying often ends in losses. Win instead on clarity and service: better explanations, honest sizing or usage advice, careful packing, a handwritten thank-you note, or quick, friendly replies on WhatsApp. Our tips on using WhatsApp Business properly will help you turn enquiries into orders instead of losing them in a messy chat list.

A sensible introductory offer can help too, as long as it is genuine and sustainable: free local delivery on first orders, for example, if your margins allow it. What matters is that the offer is real, clearly explained and honoured exactly as promised.

Keep Going Longer Than Feels Comfortable

Most new stores are not discovered in a burst; they are discovered gradually, as content is indexed, reviews accumulate and word spreads. The sellers who succeed are usually not the ones with a secret trick, but the ones who kept improving one small thing each week while others gave up. Track where each enquiry came from, repeat what works, and fix what confuses people.

Your first customers are a foundation, not a finish line. Win them honestly, serve them well, and let their real experience become the proof that brings the next customers to you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do most sellers find their very first customers?

Usually from people who already know and trust them: existing shop customers, family and community contacts, and local groups. Those first buyers matter because their honest feedback and reviews help strangers trust you later.

Should I pay for advertising straight away?

Not before your pages are ready. Advertising sends people to your store, but clear photos, honest descriptions and easy contact details are what turn visits into orders. Fix the basics first, then test small and carefully if you choose to advertise.

Is it wrong to ask friends and family to buy?

It is fine to tell people you know about your business and invite them to look. It is not right to pressure them, or to ask anyone for a review of something they did not really buy. Genuine early support is valuable; forced support is not.

What if nobody buys in the first weeks?

Do not panic or start copying tricks. Check the basics: can people find you, understand your product, see the price and contact you easily? Ask a few honest people what put them off, fix what you learn, and keep showing up consistently.
