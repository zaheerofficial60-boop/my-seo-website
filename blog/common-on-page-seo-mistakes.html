Common On-Page SEO Mistakes That Hold Websites Back

Most websites are not held back by one big disaster. They are held back by a handful of small, fixable mistakes repeated on every page. Here are the ones we see most often.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · On-Page SEO

When a business owner says their website is on Google but brings no customers, the cause is rarely mysterious. A review usually finds the same pattern: vague titles, thin content, missing headings, heavy images and pages that do not link to each other. Each mistake is small. Together, they make a site invisible in practice.

This list will help you audit your own pages honestly. Every item here is something our on-page SEO service checks as standard, and every fix is within reach of a careful site owner.

Mistake 1: Titles That Say Nothing

Titles like Home, Welcome or Services give Google no topic to rank and give searchers no reason to click. Worse, many sites repeat the same title on every page, so Google cannot tell the pages apart. The fix is simple: one unique title per page that names the service or topic first, in plain words a customer would search for.

The same problem appears in meta descriptions left empty or copied across the site. Google then invents a snippet from random page text, which rarely presents your business well. Ten minutes per page fixes both fields for good.

Mistake 2: One Page Trying to Cover Everything

A single page that lists every service in a few lines cannot rank well for any of them, because it matches no search properly. Each important service deserves focused space, either its own page or a substantial section, with its own heading and explanation.

The opposite mistake also exists: many near-identical pages created only to repeat keywords for different towns, with the same text and a swapped place name. Those pages offer visitors nothing new and look manipulative. Honest, distinct content for each topic is slower to build, but it is the only approach that lasts.

Mistake 3: Keyword Stuffing and Robotic Writing

Repeating a phrase in every sentence does not convince Google. It convinces readers that the page was not written for them. Modern search engines understand topics, related words and intent, so natural writing that covers a subject properly performs better than forced repetition.

A good test is reading a paragraph aloud. If you would never say it to a customer standing in front of you, rewrite it. Clarity, examples and direct answers are what both readers and rankings respond to now.

Mistake 4: No Headings or Chaotic Headings

Long pages without headings exhaust readers, and pages with several main titles or random heading sizes confuse search engines. Structure should be boringly logical: one main heading, major sections below it, and subsections where needed. Our guide to heading structure for SEO shows the pattern in detail.

Headings are also where many sites hide a second mistake: making ordinary sentences into headings just to use keywords. A heading should summarise the section below it, nothing more and nothing less.

Mistake 5: Heavy Images With No Descriptions

Uploading full-size phone photos slows pages, especially for mobile visitors, and images named IMG_0001 with no alt text give Google no context. Rename files descriptively, resize and compress them, and write honest alt text. Our image SEO and alt text guide walks through the routine step by step.

Slow pages lose visitors before content is even seen. In competitive local searches, the faster, clearer site often wins the enquiry even at a similar ranking, simply because it respects the visitor's time.

Mistake 6: Pages That Lead Nowhere

A page with no internal links strands the reader. They finish the page and must guess where to go next, so many simply leave. Link each page to related services and articles where it helps, and make the next step, such as calling or reading a guide, obvious.

Broken links belong in this category too. A link to a page that no longer exists tells visitors and Google that the site is neglected. Click your own links during every review and fix failures immediately.

Mistake 7: Outdated and Inconsistent Information

Old phone numbers, closed services still listed, and hours that differ between the website and the Google Business Profile quietly destroy trust. A customer who calls a dead number rarely tries a second one. Review contact details and service lists regularly, and treat accuracy as part of SEO, because for local businesses it truly is.

The encouraging point is that none of these mistakes needs a new website. They need a methodical review, page by page, starting with the pages that matter most to your income.

Work through your site in a calm order. First fix accuracy, because wrong details lose customers today. Next fix titles and headings, because they shape how every page is found and read. Then improve thin content, repair images and links, and only after that judge whether any page needs to be rebuilt completely. Keeping a short written list as you go helps you see progress, avoids fixing the same issue twice, and gives you a clear record to review again in a few months when you repeat the check.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most damaging on-page mistake?

Thin, unclear pages are usually the biggest problem because they give Google and visitors too little to work with. A page that barely explains a service cannot compete with a page that answers real questions in detail.

Is keyword stuffing still a problem?

Yes. Repeating keywords unnaturally makes text hard to read and signals low quality. Write naturally for the reader, use important terms where they fit, and focus on covering the topic properly.

How can I check my site for these mistakes?

Read each key page as a customer, check titles and headings, test on your phone, and click every internal link. For a structured review, an SEO audit checks these points systematically across the whole site.

Should I fix all mistakes at once?

Fix serious accuracy problems immediately, such as wrong contact details. For the rest, work page by page starting with your most important services, so changes stay careful and easy to review.
