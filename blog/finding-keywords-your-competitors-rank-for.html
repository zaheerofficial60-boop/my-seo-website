Finding Keywords Your Competitors Rank For

Keyword gap thinking in plain language: how to discover the searches rivals rank for, judge which matter, and turn them into your own content plan.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · SEO Audits

Every day, potential customers type searches into Google and end up on your competitors' websites instead of yours. The words they typed are not secret. With a little structured thinking, you can discover which keywords your competitors rank for and use that knowledge to plan better pages.

This article explains keyword gap thinking in plain words: what it is, how the research works, and how to turn a list of rival keywords into honest, original content of your own.

What Is a Keyword Gap, in Plain Words?

A keyword gap is a search term that a competitor ranks for and you do not. Imagine a rival plumber, tailor, or tutor appearing on Google for a service-plus-town search that your website never shows up for. That missing search is a gap.

Gaps happen for simple reasons: the rival has a page about that topic and you do not, or their page is stronger, clearer, or better supported. Finding gaps is therefore really finding missing or weak topics on your own site. This research is part of our SEO audits and competitor analysis service.

Start With the Searches That Matter

Begin by listing the searches your customers actually make: your main services, combined with your town and nearby areas like Layyah, Chobara, or Mianwali. Search them yourself and note who appears. Those businesses are your true search competitors for those terms.

Then compare, page by page. Which of those searches lead to a rival's detailed service page, while your site offers only a brief mention or nothing at all? Each mismatch is a candidate gap worth recording.

Look at the Pages Behind the Rankings

A ranking always belongs to a page, so study the page, not just the keyword. How thorough is the rival's page? What questions does it answer? Does it include process explanations, photos, or frequently asked questions that your page lacks?

  • Note the main topic and subtopics the page covers
  • See which questions it answers that yours does not
  • Check whether it targets a specific town or audience
  • Observe how it links to their other pages
  • Never copy wording; note topics, then write your own

This turns a bare keyword into a content brief. You now know what searchers are being given by the current winner, and you can aim to serve them better in your own words. The wider method sits inside competitor analysis for SEO explained.

Sort Gaps by Value, Not by Volume

Not every gap deserves a page. Some searches are irrelevant to what you actually offer, and some are asked by people who will never buy. Prioritise gaps that match your real services, your real area, and clear buying intent.

Also consider difficulty honestly. A gap where page one is filled with huge national companies may be a long-term aim, while a local service search held by a weak page is an opportunity you can act on now. Common sense beats chasing every term a tool lists.

Turn Gaps Into Your Own Content Plan

Each worthwhile gap becomes a task: a new service page, an expanded section, or a helpful article answering the questions behind the search. Write for your customers first, in your own voice, using your real experience of the work.

Over time, this is how smaller businesses close distance on bigger rivals: not by tricks, but by systematically covering what customers search for, better and more locally. Keep a written plan for these topics so nothing useful is forgotten between busy weeks.

Review Gaps as the Market Moves

Keyword gaps are not fixed forever. Rivals publish new pages, new businesses appear, and customer language changes. Revisit the comparison when you repeat your website audit, and your content plan will stay connected to the real market.

Handled this way, competitor keywords are not a source of envy. They are a free map of what your market wants, drawn by the results customers already see every day.

Checking Your Findings Before You Act

Before building a page around any gap, check the search yourself and read the top results carefully. Sometimes a term looks attractive but the results are all large directories or national brands answering a different intention than you assumed. Sometimes the opposite happens: the top page is weak, outdated, or clearly not local, which makes the opportunity better than it first appeared.

Also confirm the topic fits your real work. Ranking for a service you do not actually offer, or in a town you do not serve, brings enquiries you cannot use and can harm trust. The best gaps sit exactly where your services, your area, and customer demand overlap.

Write each confirmed gap into your plan with a note about the rival page that inspired it. That note keeps the research useful months later, when you finally sit down to write the page.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does keyword gap mean?

A keyword gap is a search term a competitor ranks for that your website does not. It usually points to a topic they cover with a page or section that your site is missing or covers only weakly.

Is it legal and fair to research competitor keywords?

Yes. Rankings and published pages are public information that anyone can view in Google results. The honest approach is to learn from the topics, then write your own original content.

Should I try to rank for every keyword a competitor has?

No. Focus on gaps that match your real services, your service area, and customers who are likely to buy. Irrelevant or extremely difficult terms are poor uses of your time.

Do I need paid tools for keyword gap research?

Paid tools make it faster, but the basic method is free: search your main service and town terms, note who ranks and with which pages, and compare their topics against your own site.
