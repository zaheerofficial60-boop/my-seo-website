Product Page SEO Guide: Titles, Descriptions, Images and Reviews

A product page has two jobs: help Google understand exactly what you sell, and help a shopper feel confident enough to buy it. This guide walks through each part of the page and how to get it right.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · Ecommerce SEO

Of all the pages in an online store, product pages are where searches turn into sales. A shopper who lands on a category page is still browsing, but a shopper on a product page is close to a decision. That is why product page SEO deserves careful attention. A good product page is clear about what the item is, honest about its details, and easy to buy from. This guide covers the four areas that matter most: titles, descriptions, images and reviews.

Work through your most important products first, using the steps below, and then apply the same pattern across the store. Our ecommerce SEO service includes this kind of page-by-page improvement for store owners who want help.

Write Titles That Name the Product Clearly

The product title is often the first thing Google and the shopper see, so it should describe the item in the words a buyer would use. Put the product type first, then the details that separate it from similar items, such as material, size, colour or model. Keep each title unique across your store. When ten products share the same vague title, Google struggles to tell them apart, and shoppers cannot either.

Avoid stuffing titles with repeated keywords or promotional phrases. A clear, specific title written for a human reader is also the title that works best in search, because it matches the way people actually search for products.

Write Descriptions That Answer Real Questions

The description is where many stores lose their advantage, because they paste the supplier's text that appears on countless other websites. Write your own instead. Explain what the product is, who it is for, how it is used, and what the buyer receives. Add the practical details shoppers look for before ordering:

  • Specifications: size, weight, material, colour options and anything that affects fit or use.
  • Care and limits: how to look after the item, and anything it is not suitable for, stated honestly.
  • Delivery and returns: what the customer can expect after ordering, in plain language.
  • Your own knowledge: a tip from experience, such as which option suits which need.

A useful way to write is to imagine a customer in your shop asking questions, and answer those questions on the page. That approach, also covered in our existing guide writing product descriptions that sell, produces text that is natural, unique and genuinely helpful.

Use Images That Inform, Not Just Decorate

Shoppers cannot touch an online product, so images do the touching for them. Show the product clearly from more than one angle, include a photo that gives a sense of scale or the item in use, and keep the images sharp but sensibly sized so pages still load well on mobile connections. Give each image a descriptive file name and alt text that says what the photo shows. Alt text helps search engines understand the image and helps shoppers who use screen readers.

Avoid using only the supplier's single small photo if you can photograph the product yourself. Your own photos make the page unique, build trust, and often answer questions a stock image cannot, such as the real colour or finish of the item.

Handle Reviews Honestly

Reviews give a product page something no store owner can write alone: the voice of previous buyers. Genuine reviews describe the product in everyday language, mention details you might forget, and reassure new shoppers. Invite customers to review their purchase, make leaving a review easy, and respond politely when someone takes the time. Never invent reviews or copy them from another site. Fake praise damages trust, and trust is the whole point of showing reviews at all.

If a product has no reviews yet, the page can still perform well with a strong title, description and images. Reviews strengthen a good page; they do not replace the basics.

Connect Each Product Page to the Rest of the Store

A product page should not be a dead end. Link it clearly to its category page, so shoppers can browse similar items, and suggest genuinely related products that pair well or offer an alternative. These internal links help Google understand the structure of your store and help visitors keep shopping instead of leaving. Also check the small essentials: the price and stock status should be accurate, the page address should be readable, and the buy button should work smoothly on a phone.

Finally, review your product pages whenever products change. Update descriptions when details change, and handle items that sell out using the approach in our guide to out-of-stock products and SEO, so good pages are not wasted.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a product description be?

Long enough to answer the shopper's real questions and no longer. Cover what the product is, who it suits, key details and care or delivery information. A short unique description is far better than a long one copied from a supplier.

Can I use the manufacturer's description on my product pages?

You can use the manufacturer's facts, such as sizes and materials, but rewrite the description in your own words. When many stores publish identical text, Google has little reason to favour your page, and shoppers get no sense of your store.

Do product reviews help SEO?

Genuine reviews add fresh, natural wording about the product and help new shoppers trust the page. Only show reviews that real customers have left, and never invent them. An honest page with no reviews is better than a page with fake praise.

What is the most common product page mistake?

The most common mistake is a thin page: a copied sentence or two, a generic title shared with other products, and one small image. Pages like this give Google and shoppers very little to work with, so they rarely perform well in search.
