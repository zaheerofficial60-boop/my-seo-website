Urdu, Roman Urdu and English: Which Language Works for SEO?

Pakistan searches in three writing styles, sometimes in the same hour. Choosing the right language for each page is one of the most practical SEO decisions a local business makes.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · SEO in Pakistan

Ask a room of Pakistani business owners what language their website should use and you will get three confident, different answers. One swears by English because it looks professional. Another insists Urdu connects with real customers. A third points out that most people actually type Roman Urdu into their phones. The honest answer is that all three are right, for different pages and different audiences.

Language is not a branding decoration in SEO. It is how a searcher phrases a question, and how Google matches that question to your page. Getting the match right matters more than looking impressive.

How People in Pakistan Actually Search

Everyday life in Pakistan moves between languages constantly. A person might read an English news headline, send Roman Urdu messages to friends, and listen to an Urdu sermon, all before lunch. Searching follows the same pattern. Formal topics, education, careers, technology and professional services are often searched in English. Everyday needs, local services, recipes, home remedies and shopping questions are often searched in Urdu script or Roman Urdu.

Voice search adds another layer, because spoken questions tend to come out in the language the person speaks at home. A business that only listens to one of these styles hears only part of its market.

When English Works Best

English is usually the safer main language for business websites in Pakistan. It suits professional services, B2B work, technology, education aimed at older students, and anything aimed at overseas Pakistanis. It is also easier to keep consistent, because spellings are standard and writers are easier to find. A clear English page with simple sentences beats a difficult English page full of heavy words every time: the goal is understanding, not showing off.

English pages also work well on a Google Business Profile, where categories and services are structured by Google itself. Our service page on local SEO and Google Business Profile optimisation shows how profiles and website pages support each other.

When Urdu Script Works Best

Urdu script carries trust and comfort for many readers, especially for religious content, news, poetry, government information and topics aimed at readers who are less comfortable in English. If your audience reads Urdu comfortably and searches in Urdu, original Urdu content can serve them better than any translation. The key word is original. Urdu content copied from other sites, or translated word by word from English, reads badly and helps no one.

Where Roman Urdu Fits

Roman Urdu is how Pakistan talks to its phones. People type product questions, prices and everyday problems in Roman Urdu because it is fast on an English keyboard. Businesses often miss this because Roman Urdu looks informal, but searchers do not care about formal. They care about answers.

  • Use natural Roman Urdu phrases inside product descriptions and FAQs where customers would phrase the question that way.
  • Answer common questions in the same mixed style customers use when they message you.
  • Do not build thin duplicate pages in Roman Urdu just to catch searches; one helpful page beats three copied ones.
  • Keep names, prices, addresses and phone numbers in clear standard form whatever the surrounding language.

Mixing Languages Without Making a Mess

Mixing is normal in Pakistan, but a website still needs structure. Keep one main language per page so the reader settles in. Use the other languages naturally inside that page where a phrase truly belongs, such as a common product name or a familiar question. Avoid switching language every second sentence, and avoid publishing the same page three times in three scripts with only the script changed. Google and readers both prefer one strong page to three weak copies.

Reviews deserve the same thinking. Customers will review you in whichever language they like, and that variety looks natural. Reply in the language of the review where you can; it shows a real person is listening.

Deciding for Your Business

Start from your customers, not from habit. Listen to how enquiries arrive: the words in messages, the questions on calls, the phrases people use at the counter. That is your search language data, collected free. A tailor, a coaching academy and a software service in the same town may honestly need three different mixes, and that is fine. Our guide to SEO in Pakistan puts language in its wider context, and the article on SEO for Pakistani small businesses shows how content choices fit a small budget.

The Simple Rule

Write so the searcher feels the page was written for them. If they searched in careful English, answer in clear English. If they asked the way people talk, let the page talk that way too, while keeping facts precise. Language choice in Pakistani SEO is not about loyalty to one script. It is about respect for how your customers actually ask.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should my whole website be in Urdu or English?

Neither choice fits every business. Decide page by page: formal and professional services often suit English, everyday local services often suit Urdu or Roman Urdu wording inside the content, and many businesses do best with clear English pages that naturally include the Roman Urdu phrases customers use.

Does Google understand Urdu content?

Yes, Google can read and index Urdu script content. What matters most is that the content is original, clearly written and genuinely helpful to the reader, whichever script it uses.

Is Roman Urdu good for SEO?

Roman Urdu matches how many people type everyday searches, so using natural Roman Urdu phrases inside your content can help you match those searches. It works best as natural wording, not as a separate copied version of the same page.

Should I translate every page into both languages?

Only if you can do it properly and keep both versions updated. A few well-written pages in the right language for each audience are far better than a full site of rushed, copied translations.
