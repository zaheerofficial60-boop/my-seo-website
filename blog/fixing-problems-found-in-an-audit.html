Fixing Problems Found in an SEO Audit

How to work through audit findings calmly and in the right order, so the most damaging problems are fixed first and every fix is verified.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · SEO Audits

Receiving an audit report can feel daunting, especially if the list of problems is long. The good news is that audit findings are not meant to be fixed all at once, and they are certainly not meant to panic you. They are meant to be worked through in a sensible order.

This guide explains how to prioritise and fix what an audit finds, which jobs most owners can handle themselves, and which ones are worth handing to a specialist.

First, Sort Findings Into Three Groups

Start by dividing the report into three groups. Group one is anything broken or harmful: pages Google cannot reach, wrong phone numbers, broken contact forms, security warnings. These come first because they actively lose you customers.

Group two is important improvements: thin service pages, missing titles, slow key pages, an incomplete Google Business Profile. Group three is polish and long-term ideas: extra content, refinements, and gradual link building. This ordering is the heart of our SEO audits and competitor analysis service, because order matters as much as the list itself.

Fix Technical Blockers Before Anything Else

Technical blockers stop everything else from working. If Google cannot index a page, improving its text achieves nothing yet. If your contact form silently fails, more visitors just means more lost enquiries.

  • Restore or redirect missing important pages
  • Fix indexing and crawling problems
  • Repair broken links, forms, and phone links
  • Correct your name, address, and phone everywhere
  • Resolve any security or penalty warnings properly

Some of these fixes need your website developer or an SEO specialist, and that is normal. Ask for each fix to be explained and verified, not just marked done.

Then Improve Your Most Important Pages

With blockers cleared, work on the pages that matter most commercially: your homepage, main services, and location pages. Expand thin content so it genuinely answers customer questions. Give every page a clear title and heading, and link related pages together helpfully.

Do not try to perfect every page in a week. Steady, page-by-page improvement beats a rushed rewrite, and it lets you watch what changes. For writing guidance, see our on-page SEO checklist.

Handle Links and Listings Carefully

If the audit found inconsistent listings, correct them one by one, keeping a note of where you have updated. If it found poor-quality links pointing to your site, be cautious: link problems need a careful, experienced response, not hasty mass action that can make things worse.

On the positive side, the audit may have listed honest link opportunities: local organisations, suppliers, and community sites connected to your work. Pursue those gradually and naturally, as part of real relationships, not as a bulk exercise.

Track Fixes and Give Them Time

Keep a simple record: the finding, the fix, and the date. Then be patient. Search engines need time to recrawl your site and reassess pages, so judge results over weeks, not days. Check Search Console to confirm fixed pages are being indexed as expected.

This record also protects you. If a problem returns, or a future redesign is planned, you have a history of what was done and why, as recommended in how often to audit your website.

Know When a Finding Is Not Worth Fixing

Finally, not every line in every report deserves equal effort. Minor warnings on unimportant pages, or generic suggestions that do not fit your business, can be consciously set aside. A good audit discussion includes what to ignore, not only what to do.

The goal is a healthier website and more enquiries, not a perfect score. Work in order, verify each fix, and let steady progress do its work.

A Realistic Order of Work for a Small Business

For a typical small business, a sensible sequence looks like this. In the first week, deal with access and contact problems: indexing faults, wrong numbers, broken forms, and any security warnings. In the following weeks, work through the main service pages one at a time, improving titles, headings, and content, and complete your Google Business Profile properly.

After that, move to the steady work: correcting listings, adding missing pages for important services, and pursuing honest local mentions. Spread across a few months, this pace is manageable alongside running a business, and each stage builds on a foundation that is already fixed.

Ask whoever helps you to confirm each stage in writing. A short note saying what changed, where, and when is enough, and it becomes part of the audit history that makes your next review quicker and cheaper.

Remember also to re-test the things customers touch most after any round of fixes: the phone links, the contact form, the main menu, and your most important service page on a real phone. Fixes sometimes disturb neighbouring parts of a site, and a two-minute check protects the enquiries the whole exercise was meant to win back.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to fix everything an audit finds?

No. Findings should be prioritised. Broken and harmful issues come first, important improvements next, and minor polish last. Some low-value suggestions can reasonably be set aside after discussion.

How long do fixes take to affect my rankings?

It varies. Google needs time to recrawl and reassess your pages, so judge over weeks rather than days. Verifying fixes in Search Console helps confirm Google has seen the changes.

Can I fix audit problems myself?

Many can: correcting details, improving content, completing your profile, and fixing simple links. Technical issues like indexing faults or redirects are often better handled by a specialist.

What should I do if the report overwhelms me?

Ask for the findings in priority order and tackle group one only. A good provider will happily explain the order and help you stage the work at a pace you can manage.
