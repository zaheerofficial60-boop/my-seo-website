Keyword Difficulty Explained: How to Judge It Honestly

A difficulty score is a guess, not a verdict. Learn what really makes a keyword hard, and how to judge your chances by looking at the results yourself.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · Keyword Research

Keyword difficulty is an estimate of how hard it would be for a new or small website to appear on the first page for a particular search. Tools express it as a score, but the score is not a fact about Google. It is a prediction, made by looking at the strength of the pages that already rank. Understanding what sits behind that prediction helps you judge keywords honestly instead of either fearing every competitive phrase or wasting months on impossible ones.

Think of difficulty as a queue. For some searches, the front of the queue is held by huge brands, government sites, and famous directories with years of trust. For other searches, the front is held by ordinary local businesses with modest websites. Your chances depend far less on the keyword itself than on who is already standing in that queue.

What Makes a Keyword Difficult?

Several factors combine. The authority of the ranking websites matters most: large, trusted sites with many other sites linking to them are hard to displace. The quality and completeness of their pages matters too. If the top results answer the search thoroughly, a thin page will not pass them. The type of results also counts. A results page filled with map listings, shopping blocks, and big directories leaves fewer ordinary website spots to win.

Intent plays a hidden role. Transactional phrases attract businesses willing to invest heavily, because those searches bring money. Informational phrases attract publishers and educators. Either way, valuable keywords attract strong competition, while overlooked phrases, often the specific local and long-tail ones, remain within reach of a small site that serves the searcher well.

Why Difficulty Scores Differ Between Tools

Each tool builds its score from its own data and its own formula, so the same keyword can look easy in one tool and hard in another. Neither is reading Google mind. Both are guessing from the visible strength of current results. This is why a thoughtful keyword research and strategy service treats scores as a starting hint, then checks the actual results page by eye before making promises or plans.

The honest way to judge difficulty is to search the phrase yourself and study the first page like a customer. Ask: who are these sites? Are they national giants or businesses like mine? How good are their pages, really? Could I honestly make something more helpful for this specific searcher? Those answers tell you more than any single number.

A Practical Way to Judge Any Keyword

  1. Search the phrase and list the types of sites on page one.
  2. Count how many are large brands, directories, or national sites.
  3. Open the top three ordinary results and judge their quality honestly.
  4. Check whether local or small-business results appear at all.
  5. Decide: target now, target a more specific version, or leave for later.

If page one contains even a few businesses similar to yours, the keyword is probably reachable with a better page and steady work. If it is wall-to-wall giants with excellent content, respect the evidence and choose a narrower phrase: add your town, your speciality, or the specific problem you solve. Narrowing a keyword is not surrender. It is aiming at the customers you can actually win, as explained in long-tail keywords explained.

Difficulty Is Not Fixed Forever

A keyword that is too hard today may be realistic next year. Websites grow in authority as they publish helpful content, earn links, and collect reviews. A sensible strategy therefore works in stages: win the specific phrases first, let that success build your site strength, then challenge broader phrases from a stronger position. Trying to reverse the order usually ends in frustration and abandoned websites.

Difficulty also varies by place. A service phrase that is fiercely contested in a big city may be wide open in a smaller town where few businesses have proper websites. Local research often reveals these quiet opportunities, which is why town-level businesses should always judge difficulty on their own results pages rather than trusting a generic national score.

How to Use Difficulty in Your Planning

Sort your keyword list into three honest piles: phrases you can target now, phrases to grow toward, and phrases to drop because they do not fit your business at any stage. Put your effort into the first pile, keep the second pile visible as motivation, and do not let the third pile distract you. Revisit the piles every few months as your site strengthens. Frequently Asked Questions

What does keyword difficulty mean?

Keyword difficulty is an estimate of how hard it would be for a smaller website to rank on the first page for a search, based mainly on the strength of the pages already ranking there.

Can I trust keyword difficulty scores from tools?

Only as a rough hint. Each tool uses its own data and formula, so scores differ. Always check the real first page yourself before deciding whether a keyword is realistic.

How do I judge difficulty without any tool?

Search the phrase and study page one. If businesses similar to yours appear with ordinary pages, it is likely reachable. If only national giants appear, choose a more specific phrase.

What should I do if a keyword is too difficult?

Narrow it by adding your town, your speciality, or the specific problem you solve, target that version first, and revisit the broader phrase as your website grows stronger.
