Guest Posting for Backlinks: The Honest Way to Do It

Guest posting can earn relevant links and new readers, or it can waste money on sites nobody visits. This guide shows the honest approach: real sites, useful articles, natural links and patience.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · Off-Page SEO

Guest posting means writing an article for someone else's website, with a link back to your own site included naturally, usually in the article or the author bio. Done honestly, it is one of the most respectable ways to earn links, because you give real value first: a useful article for their readers. Done badly, it becomes a shortcut industry of paid placements on sites nobody reads, which helps no one.

This guide explains the honest way: how to find suitable sites, what to offer, how to write something worth publishing, and how to avoid the traps that turn guest posting into a waste of money or a risk to your site.

Why Guest Posting Works When It Is Genuine

A good guest post does three things at once. It earns a relevant link from a real website. It puts your name and expertise in front of new readers who may become customers. And it builds a relationship with another site owner in your field, which can lead to further mentions over time. Those benefits only exist when the host site has genuine readers and editorial standards.

That is why our off-page SEO and link building service treats guest posting as publishing, not link buying. The article must be good enough that the host site is glad to have it, and the link must sit naturally inside useful content rather than being forced into unrelated text.

Finding the Right Sites

Start with relevance. A site about your trade, your town or a closely related topic is worth far more than a general site that publishes anything. Look for signs of a real audience: recent articles, comments or shares, a named editor, and content that is clearly written for readers rather than stuffed with paid posts.

  • Search for blogs and magazines in your industry or local area.
  • Check sites that already link to your competitors for ideas.
  • Avoid sites where most articles are clearly paid placements.
  • Prefer a modest relevant site over a large irrelevant one.

Pitching Without Sounding Like Spam

Editors receive piles of identical guest post requests, and most go straight to the bin. Yours should be short, personal and specific. Show that you have read the site by naming a recent article. Suggest two or three concrete topics their readers would value, and briefly say why you are qualified to write on them. Offer to follow their guidelines and accept edits.

Never offer money for a link while pretending it is a free contribution, and never send the finished article before the editor agrees. If a site replies with a price list for links, think carefully. Paying for placement purely for ranking value goes against Google's guidelines, and many such sites are low quality link sellers in disguise.

Writing an Article Worth Publishing

Write for the host site's readers, not for your own link. Cover the topic thoroughly, use clear headings, and include practical detail the reader can act on. Mention your own business sparingly, if at all, in the body. The link usually belongs where it genuinely helps, such as a reference to a fuller guide on your site, or in the author bio the editor provides.

Keep the anchor text, the clickable words of your link, natural. Your name, your business name, or a plain description of the page works well. Repeating an exact sales keyword in every guest post looks manipulative and is one of the patterns discussed in our guide to link building mistakes to avoid.

After Publication

When your article goes live, thank the editor, share the article with your own audience, and reply to any comments. Check the link works and points to the right page. Record the placement in a simple spreadsheet so your link profile stays organised. Over time, a set of thoughtful guest posts on relevant sites builds the kind of reputation search engines and readers both respect.

Red Flags to Walk Away From

Walk away from sites that accept any topic, publish dozens of unrelated posts a day, hide who runs them, or guarantee a link before seeing your writing. Walk away from anyone selling guest posts in bulk packages. Real publishing takes time: research, writing, editing and waiting. If the process feels instant and effortless, it is not real publishing, and the link will not be worth having.

Guest posting done patiently is slow, but it compounds. Each good article keeps working for you, bringing readers, trust and a link that makes sense to everyone who sees it, including Google.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is guest posting in SEO?

Guest posting means writing a useful article for another website in your field, which publishes it with a link back to your site, often in the content or author bio. The value comes from the host site being real, relevant and read by genuine visitors.

Should I pay for guest post placements?

Be careful. Paying purely for a link that passes ranking value goes against Google's guidelines, and many sites selling placements are low quality. Genuine guest posting is earned by offering an article the editor actually wants to publish.

How do I find sites that accept guest posts?

Look for blogs and magazines in your trade or local area with recent, quality content and a real audience. Suggest specific topics in a short, personal email that shows you have read their site and understand their readers.

Where should the link go in a guest post?

Place it where it genuinely helps the reader, such as a reference to a fuller guide, or in the author bio. Use natural anchor text like your business name. Forcing sales keywords into every link looks manipulative and can cause harm.
