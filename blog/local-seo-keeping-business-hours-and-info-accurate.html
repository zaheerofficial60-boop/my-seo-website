Keeping Business Hours and Info Accurate for Local Customers

Customers often check a business online before they visit or call. If the hours, phone number or address shown online are wrong, they may go elsewhere or arrive at the wrong time. Keeping your basic business information accurate is one of the simplest and most useful parts of local SEO.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 28 October 2026 · local SEO and Google Business Profile

Why Accurate Information Matters

When a customer searches for a service in their town, they want a clear answer to a simple question: is this business open, where is it, and how can I contact it. If that information is correct, the customer can act with confidence. If it is out of date, even a good business can lose a visit or a phone call. Accurate details also help search engines understand your business and show it for the right local searches.

Trust grows from small details. A correct phone number, a clear address and honest opening hours tell a customer that the business is active and careful. On the other hand, a wrong number or an old address can create doubt before any conversation begins. For a small business, that first impression often happens online, not in person, so the online details deserve the same care as a shop sign.

Accuracy is not a one-time task. Businesses change their hours for holidays, move to a new location, change a phone number or add a new service. Each change should be reflected online as soon as possible. A short regular check can prevent confusion and keep customers from wasting a trip.

Start With Your Opening Hours

Opening hours are one of the first things customers check, especially when they plan a visit in the evening or on a weekend. Make sure the hours shown on your Google Business Profile match the hours you actually keep. If you are open around the clock, say so clearly. If you close for lunch or on a certain day, that detail should also be clear and correct.

Special days need extra attention. Public holidays, religious occasions and local events can change normal hours. When you know about a change in advance, update your hours early so customers can plan. If a change is sudden, update it as soon as you can and, where possible, add a short post explaining the change. Customers are usually understanding when they are told in advance.

Do not leave old holiday hours in place after the holiday has passed. After a special period ends, return to your normal hours right away. A quick check at the start of each week can help you catch mistakes before customers do. Keeping a simple note of your normal and special hours makes this task easier.

Keep Your Name, Address and Phone Consistent

Your business name, address and phone number should look the same everywhere they appear online. Small differences, such as a different spelling of a road name or an old phone number on one listing, can confuse customers and search engines. Choose one standard form for your name and address and use it consistently on your profile, website and other listings.

Your phone number deserves special care. If you change your number, update it everywhere at once, including your website footer, your business profile and any directory listings you control. Ask a friend or team member to test the new number by calling it, so you know customers can reach you. A number that rings correctly is a basic but important part of good service.

Your address should be complete and easy to follow. Include the road, area and town in a clear order, so a customer using maps can find you without extra help. If your location is inside a larger building or market, a short landmark note can help, as long as it is accurate. Avoid vague directions that only a local regular would understand.

Update Services and Descriptions Honestly

Customers also want to know what you actually offer. A short, honest description of your services helps the right customers contact you and saves time for everyone. List the services you provide now, not services you plan to offer later or services you no longer provide. Clear wording is better than long lists filled with terms customers do not use.

When you add a new service, add it to your profile and website at the same time. When you stop offering a service, remove it. This keeps expectations realistic and avoids disappointing a customer who travelled or called for something you no longer do. If a service has changed in scope, update the description in plain words so customers understand what is included.

Avoid exaggeration in your description. Do not promise results, speed or outcomes you cannot control. Instead, explain what you do, who you help and how a customer can contact you to discuss their needs. Honest descriptions build trust and lead to better conversations, because customers arrive with a clear idea of what to expect.

Use Photos and Posts to Support Accuracy

Photos help customers recognise your business and feel confident about visiting. Use recent photos of your shopfront, workspace or team where appropriate, and make sure they reflect how the business looks now. If you have moved, changed your sign or renovated, replace old photos so customers are not misled by an outdated image.

Short posts can also support accuracy. A post about new hours, a new service or a change of location gives customers current information in a timely way. Keep posts short, clear and factual, and avoid claims you cannot support. A simple update is often more helpful than a long promotional message.

Check your photos and posts from time to time and remove anything that is clearly out of date. Old photos of a previous location or a service you no longer offer can confuse new customers. A small, current set of photos is better than a large collection that no longer matches reality.

Build a Simple Routine to Stay Accurate

You do not need a complicated system to keep information accurate. A short monthly check is enough for most small businesses. Once a month, open your business profile and website and compare the name, address, phone number, hours and services with what is true today. Fix anything that does not match, even if the difference seems small.

Keep a simple record of changes. When you change hours, a phone number or a service, note the date and what changed. This record helps you remember what was updated and where, and it makes the next check faster. If more than one person manages your online presence, agree on who is responsible for updates so nothing is missed.

Finally, listen to customers. If a customer says they could not find you, called an old number or arrived when you were closed, treat that as useful feedback. Check the source of the wrong information and correct it. Customers often notice mistakes before the business does, and fixing them quickly shows care and professionalism.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I check my business information online?

A short check once a month is a good habit for most small businesses, with extra checks before holidays or after any change.

What should I update first when my hours change?

Update your Google Business Profile and website hours first, since customers often check those places before visiting or calling.

Why does the same phone number need to appear everywhere?

Consistent contact details help customers reach you easily and help search engines connect your listings to the same business.

Should I remove a service I no longer offer?

Yes, removing it avoids confusion and helps customers contact you only for services you can actually provide.
