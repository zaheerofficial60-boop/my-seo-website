Link Building Mistakes to Avoid: Bought Links, Spam and Anchor Abuse

Cheap link packages and clever shortcuts cause real damage every year. Learn the link building mistakes that waste money or harm rankings, and the simple test that keeps your website safe.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · Off-Page SEO

Link building has a deserved reputation for going wrong. Every year, business owners pay for link packages that do nothing, or worse, leave their websites worse off than before. The good news is that the dangerous mistakes are well known and easy to avoid once you can recognise them. This guide walks through the main ones, from bought links to anchor text abuse, so you can protect your site and your budget.

None of this requires technical knowledge. Most link building mistakes share one feature: they try to make a website look popular instead of actually becoming worth recommending. Google has spent years learning to tell the difference.

Mistake One: Buying Links in Bulk

The classic trap is an offer of hundreds or thousands of backlinks for a small fee. These links come from networks of low quality sites created only to sell links, or from automated spam. Google either ignores them, in which case you wasted money, or treats them as manipulation, in which case your rankings can fall and recovery is slow and difficult.

Paying for links that pass ranking value breaks Google's guidelines. That does not mean all paid promotion is wrong: advertising clearly marked as advertising is normal business. The problem is disguised paid links pretending to be genuine recommendations. Our off-page SEO and link building service never uses them, and neither should you.

Mistake Two: Spam Comments and Forum Drops

Pasting your link into blog comments, forums and unrelated pages across the internet is an old tactic that fools nobody now. These links are usually marked so they pass no ranking value, the comments get deleted, and your business name becomes associated with spam. In your own community, that reputational damage can cost more than any ranking drop.

Mistake Three: Anchor Text Abuse

Anchor text is the clickable wording of a link. When every link to your site uses the exact same sales phrase, such as a service name plus a town, it looks engineered, because natural links do not happen that way. Real people link using your business name, your name, a page title, or plain phrases like read more or this guide.

  • A natural link profile mixes business names, plain phrases and page titles.
  • Exact sales keywords repeated across many links are a warning sign.
  • If you control the link, choose wording a normal person would write.

Mistake Four: Chasing Irrelevant Links

A link should make sense to a human reader. Links from sites with no connection to your trade or area, such as a plumbing page on an unrelated foreign directory, add little and can look suspicious in bulk. Relevance is one of the clearest quality signals, as explained in our guide on how to judge backlink quality. Ten relevant links will usually outperform a hundred random ones.

Mistake Five: Link Exchanges and Schemes

Arrangements where groups of sites link to each other purely to inflate rankings, sometimes called link farms or private blog networks, are another known scheme. They often work briefly, which is why they keep being sold, and then they are detected, which is why sites using them suddenly disappear from results. A normal, occasional link between genuine partner businesses is fine. A system built only to trade links is not.

Mistake Six: Expecting Instant Results

Impatience causes many of the other mistakes. Owners who demand results in days get pushed towards aggressive tactics, because honest work cannot move that fast. Links need to be discovered, assessed and trusted over time. Sudden spikes of new links to a small site look unnatural and invite scrutiny. A steady pace that matches a real business doing real things is both safer and more believable.

How to Stay Safe

Ask one question of any link opportunity: would this link still make sense if search engines did not exist? A supplier listing you, a customer recommending your guide, a local paper covering your work: all pass the test. A paid placement on a site nobody reads fails it. Keep that test, build slowly, and your link profile will be an asset instead of a liability waiting to be discovered.

Mistake Seven: Ignoring the Links You Already Have

The final mistake is neglect. Many businesses already carry old links from previous experiments, former providers or directories they joined years ago and forgot. Reviewing your link profile once in a while, using the free link report in Google Search Console, shows you what is pointing at your site. Most links will be harmless or helpful. If you find obvious patterns of spam that you did not create, do not panic; competitors and random sites attract some junk naturally, and Google ignores most of it. What matters is that you never add to the problem yourself, and that any provider you hire can explain, in plain words, every link they build for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are bought backlinks always harmful?

Links bought purely to manipulate rankings break Google's guidelines. At best Google ignores them and the money is wasted. At worst your site loses visibility and recovery takes a long time. Advertising that is clearly marked as advertising is a different, legitimate thing.

What is anchor text abuse?

It is using the exact same sales keyword phrase as the clickable text on many links to your site. Natural links use varied wording such as your business name or a page title. Repetition of one commercial phrase looks engineered and can trigger problems.

Can I recover from bad links built in the past?

Often, yes, but it takes work. The harmful links need to be identified, removal requested where possible, and the rest disavowed through Google's tools, alongside building genuine links. Prevention is far easier than recovery, so avoid risky tactics from the start.

How can I tell if a link offer is a scam?

Warning signs include guaranteed rankings, huge numbers of links for a tiny price, secrecy about which sites will link to you, and results promised within days. Honest providers explain their methods openly and set gradual, realistic expectations.
