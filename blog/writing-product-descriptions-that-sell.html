Writing Product Descriptions That Sell and Rank

Complete, honest product descriptions do two jobs at once: they help buyers decide with confidence and help your pages appear on Google. Here is how to write them.

By Zaheer SEO Expert · 27 October 2026 · Content Strategy

A product description has a difficult job. It must help the product appear on Google, answer the questions a buyer would ask in a shop, and give that buyer enough confidence to order or visit. Yet many online sellers copy the supplier's lines, write two short sentences, or list features without explaining why any of them matter. The result is a page that neither ranks nor sells.

Whether you sell clothes, electronics, furniture or farm supplies from Layyah or any nearby town, the principles in this guide will help you write descriptions that work. They are part of what our content strategy and copywriting service and ecommerce work focus on every day: clear, original, customer-focused words.

Describe the Benefit Behind Every Feature

A feature is what a product has; a benefit is what that does for the buyer. A steel jug with a wide mouth is easier to wash. A shirt in breathable fabric stays comfortable in Punjab's summer heat. Buyers rarely translate features into benefits themselves, especially when comparing several similar products quickly on a phone.

Go through your description feature by feature and add the so-what for each one. Keep the language concrete and honest. If a benefit is a guess rather than something you know about the product, leave it out; believable descriptions sell more than exaggerated ones, and they produce fewer returns and complaints.

Answer the Questions Buyers Ask in a Shop

Imagine a customer holding the product in your shop. They would ask about size, material, colour, weight, care, delivery, what it suits and what it does not suit. Your description should answer those same questions before they are asked. Every unanswered question is a reason to delay, message a competitor, or abandon the purchase entirely.

  • Exact sizes or measurements, in units customers understand.
  • Material and build details that affect daily use.
  • Colour and design options that are actually available.
  • Care, washing, charging or maintenance instructions.
  • Who the product suits best, and any honest limitations.
  • Delivery areas, and how to order or ask a question.

This information also helps search performance, because buyers search with these details. A page that mentions the material, size and use people are searching for stands a better chance of being found than a page with a name and a price alone. Write Original Descriptions, Not Supplier Copies

When dozens of sellers paste the same supplier text, search engines see many identical pages and have little reason to show yours. Customers, meanwhile, learn nothing about why they should buy from you rather than the shop in the next town. Original descriptions are one of the simplest advantages an independent seller can create.

You do not need literary talent. Describe the product as you would to a customer you respect: what it is, what it is made of, how it feels or performs in real use, and anything you have noticed from selling it. Mentioning local realities, such as heat, dust, load shedding or hard water where relevant, makes a description unmistakably yours.

Structure Descriptions for Phone Readers

Most buyers will read on a phone, often while comparing tabs. Open with two or three sentences that summarise who the product is for and its main strength. Follow with short sections or a clear list covering specifications, use and care. Save long storytelling for your blog; a product page should respect a buyer who is close to deciding.

Use the product's real name and the words buyers use in the title and first lines, naturally. Add a clear ordering step at the end: how to call, message or visit, and which towns you deliver to, such as Layyah, Chowk Azam and Karor Lal Eason if you serve them.

Build Trust Around the Description

Words sell better when the page around them feels safe. Clear photos that match the description, honest notes about variations in colour or handmade finish, and straightforward delivery and return information all reduce hesitation. If you offer checking on delivery or easy exchange for genuine faults, say so plainly; practical reassurance outperforms bold promises.

Avoid pressure language and invented scarcity. Shoppers in small towns talk to each other, and a reputation for honest descriptions brings repeat orders that no advertisement can buy. Accuracy also protects you: a customer who received exactly what was described rarely argues.

Improve Descriptions Using Real Feedback

Your best editing notes come from customers. Keep a list of questions buyers message you about each product; every repeated question marks a gap in the description. Add the answer, and the next buyer decides faster with less messaging. Similarly, if a product is often returned for a misunderstanding, the description, not the customer, usually needs fixing.

Review your best-selling and worst-selling products first. Often a weak description, not a weak product, explains poor sales. Good product descriptions are not clever writing. They are complete, honest answers given at the exact moment a buyer is deciding. Write those, product by product, and your shop pages will both rank better and sell better.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a product description be?

Long enough to answer the buyer's real questions: what it is, size and material, how it is used and cared for, who it suits and how to order. For simple products that may be a short summary plus a clear list of details.

Can I use the supplier's description on my shop?

It is better not to. Many sellers using identical text gives Google no reason to rank your page and tells customers nothing about your shop. Rewrite descriptions in your own words using your real knowledge of the product.

Should product descriptions focus on features or benefits?

Both, connected together. State the feature, then explain what it means for the buyer in daily use. Features alone leave customers guessing; benefits alone without facts sound like empty claims.

How do I improve a description that is not selling?

Check the questions buyers keep asking you about that product and add the missing answers. Also verify the title uses the words buyers search for, the details are complete, and the ordering step is obvious.
